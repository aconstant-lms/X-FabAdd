CLASS A FAILURE · RAIL · 1998

Eschede: a wheel tyre breaks at 200 km/h

101

deaths, 88 seriously injured: the world's worst high-speed rail accident

  • 3 June 1998, ICE 884 Munich → Hamburg
  • The steel tyre of a rubber-sprung wheel breaks by fatigue, peels off and pierces the floor
  • 6 km later it catches a set of points: coaches derail and hit a road bridge, which collapses on the train

The accident site (Richard et al. 2005, fig. 1)

(a) monobloc, (b) rubber-sprung wheel, Ø 920 mm (Kubota & Hirakawa 2009)

The rubber-sprung wheel BA 064

  • Steel tyre on 34 pre-compressed rubber blocks, bolted to the wheel disc by a lateral ring
  • Used for decades on trams and metros; admitted on ICE 1 trailer cars in autumn 1992 to cut vibration and noise
Richard, Fulland, Sander, Kullmer, Fracture in a rubber-sprung railway wheel, Engineering Failure Analysis 12 (2005) 986-999: the wheel broke about 6 km before the switch near Eschede; ICE wheel = tyre, 34 rubber blocks, wheel rim in two parts (centre and detachable ring) and solid shaft; permitted by Deutsche Bahn for high-speed trains in autumn 1992. Kubota and Hirakawa, Tribology International 42 (2009) 1389-1398: rubber-sprung wheels on the trailer cars, 34 rubber pads between wheel disc and tyre.
