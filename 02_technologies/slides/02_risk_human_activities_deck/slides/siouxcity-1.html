CLASS A FAILURE · AVIATION · 1989

Sioux City: one fan disc, three hydraulic systems lost

111

deaths among 296 on board; 185 survived

  • 19 July 1989, United 232, cruise at 11 300 m
  • The 168 kg titanium stage 1 fan disc of the tail engine bursts
  • Debris cuts the lines of all three hydraulic systems: no flight controls
  • The crew steers with engine thrust alone to Sioux City

Lesson: three separate hydraulic systems, routed close together: one high-energy event defeated the redundancy.

CF6-6 fan rotor and stage 1 fan disc section; disc height 259 mm, bore radius 141 mm (NTSB AAR-90/06, fig. 5, 17)

Reconstructed No. 2 fan disc (FAA Lessons Learned)

Found 3 months later in farm fields near Alta, Iowa: two pieces making up nearly the whole disc.

NTSB AAR-90/06: 285 passengers and 11 crew; 110 passengers and 1 flight attendant fatally injured. Cruise at FL370 (11 300 m). The stage 1 fan disk weighs 370 lb (168 kg), machined from a Ti-6Al-4V forging. Probable cause (NTSB): inadequate consideration of human factors limitations in the inspection and quality control procedures at the United engine overhaul facility, which failed to detect a fatigue crack from a previously undetected metallurgical defect in a critical area of the disc made by GE; debris exceeded the protection of the hydraulic system design. NTSB drawings are dimensioned in inches: 10.2 in = 259 mm, 5.56 in = 141 mm. About 3 months after the accident two sections making up nearly the entire disc were found near Alta, Iowa; the small one departed to the left, the rest to the right.
