WHERE WE STAND

2026: in series production, but not yet everywhere

In service today

  • LEAP: 10,000+ engines, 100 M+ flight hours on printed nozzles
  • Airbus: 25,000+ polymer parts a year; wire-DED Ti parts in A350 production
  • Whole engines: GE9X (2020) and Catalyst (2025) certified with printed parts
  • Investment: GE $115 M for metal AM in Cincinnati, €110 M in Europe (2026); Safran campus scaling
  • MRO: airlines and depots print certified spares (Etihad, US Navy)

What is still missing

  • Qualified material data: AM design allowables still lag forgings and castings
  • Few approved materials; standards not yet harmonised worldwide (ISO/TC 261)
  • Cost and scale: machines, powders, post-processing; testing on every programme
  • Finish and tolerances: printed parts still need machining (hybrid)
  • Large and critical parts: wire DED only now entering structure; wings and landing gear later
  • Speed and skills: qualification bottlenecks and workforce gaps (AIAA 2026)

Market size 2026: estimates range from $2.7 bn to $6.4 bn depending on the analyst (GMI, Meticulous Research).
