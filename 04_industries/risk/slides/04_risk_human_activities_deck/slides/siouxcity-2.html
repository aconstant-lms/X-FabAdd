CLASS A FAILURE · AVIATION · 1989

Sioux City: a melt defect, six inspections, one missed crack

Cycles since new and the six fluorescent penetrant inspections (NTSB AAR-90/06, fig. 9)

  • 1971: a nitrogen-rich hard alpha inclusion forms while melting the titanium (double vacuum melt)
  • A 1.4 × 0.4 mm cavity in the bore starts a fatigue crack, 31.5 mm long and 14.2 mm deep at failure
  • Six inspections accepted the disc, the last one 760 flights before the accident

15 503 cycles

at failure, against a life limit of 18 000

Why it was missed: a defect born at the melt shop, a safe-life rule that assumes defect-free material, and a manual inspection that depends on the inspector's eye. Safe life alone was not enough.

NTSB AAR-90/06 sections 1.6 and 1.17: disc S/N MPO 00385 made at GE Evendale from September to December 1971 (heat K8283); inspected by FPI in 1972, 1973, 1976, 1978, 1982 and February 1988; 41,009 hours and 15,503 cycles at the accident. Crack sizes converted from the report: fatigue zone 1.24 x 0.56 in = 31.5 x 14.2 mm; discoloured area 12.1 x 4.6 mm; cavity 0.055 x 0.015 in = 1.4 x 0.4 mm. FAA Lessons Learned describes the missed crack as about half an inch (13 mm) at the bore surface.
