ISO 26262 covers electrical and electronic systems only. Wheels, brake discs and suspension arms go through UNECE type approval, FMEA and safety-critical part marking.

Ten times looser than aircraft: the driver can often recover, a crash involves few people — but 1.5 billion cars turn 10⁻⁸ into real events. Worst recalls were mechanical: GM ignition switch (2014), Takata inflators.

ISO 26262, functional safety of road vehicles. ASIL from the hazard analysis: S (S0-S3), E (E0-E4), C (C0-C3). Random hardware failure targets (PMHF, ISO 26262-5): ASIL D < 1e-8/h, ASIL C < 1e-7/h, ASIL B < 1e-7/h, ASIL A none. GM ignition switch: low switch torque let the key slip to accessory, cutting power steering and airbags; more than 100 deaths linked. Takata: ammonium-nitrate inflators rupturing; the largest recall in history. Road risk overall is about 1e-7 per vehicle-hour, mostly driver error rather than part failure.
