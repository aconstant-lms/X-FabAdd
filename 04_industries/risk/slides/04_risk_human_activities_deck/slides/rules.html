MEASURING RISK

How the numbers are estimated, and rules of thumb

Estimating a rate

1

Count the events

Deaths, or device failures, over a period: accident statistics, clinical registries.

2

Divide by the exposure

Persons × hours: travel surveys, flight hours, ski days, implant-years × 8 766 h.

3

Convert and bracket

Per km or per trip → per hour with the average speed or duration; keep a range, not one number.

1 micromort (10⁻⁶) ≈ 370–600 km by car, 1 600 km by jet, 10 000 km by train

Lifetime = rate × hours: 1 h a day for 60 years ≈ 2 × 10⁴ h

Class A design target 10⁻⁹ per flight hour: 1 000 × below everyday life

How the chart was estimated: number of deaths (or failures) divided by the exposure in person-hours, from accident statistics (ETSC, UK DfT), sport federations (BSAC, USPA), registries (ABSORB III, joint registries) and NASA. When sources give risk per km or per trip, divide by the average speed or trip duration. Rules of thumb: 10^-8 per hour train, 10^-7 car and airliner, 10^-6 everyday life (24 micromorts per day), 10^-5 motorbike and scuba, 10^-2 launch to orbit. One micromort is about 370 km (UK, Spiegelhalter 2009) to 600 km (current UK figure) by car, 1 600 km by jet, 10 000 km by train.
