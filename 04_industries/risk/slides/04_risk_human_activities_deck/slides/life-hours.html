Check with everyday life. The average risk of dying, all causes, is about 24 micromorts a day, that is 1 × 10⁻⁶ per hour. Summed over a life: 7 × 10⁵ h × 10⁻⁶ /h ≈ 0.7, of order one — the hourly scale is consistent with one life.

Risk is counted per person and per hour of use. Lifetime risk = risk per hour × hours of exposure in a life. Assumptions (orders of magnitude): skiing one week of 30 h a year for 50 years; scuba 20 one-hour dives a year for 30 years; an implant working 24 h a day for 15 years; car 1 h a day from 18 to 80; train 100 h a year; airline 20 h a year for 60 years. Rates: ETSC (EU) and UK DfT figures per 1e8 or 1e9 person-hours; ski 0.7 micromort per ski day; scuba 5-10 micromorts per dive (BSAC); stent thrombosis 0.7 %/year (Xience, ABSORB III); hip revision about 5 % at 10 years (joint registries). Check: average all-cause mortality is about 24 micromorts per day, 1e-6 per hour; over 7e5 hours this gives 0.7. Medical numbers are device failure events, not deaths. Space: crew ascent target 1/500 in about 10 minutes (about 1e-2 per hour); Shuttle 2 losses in 135 missions of about 250 h.
