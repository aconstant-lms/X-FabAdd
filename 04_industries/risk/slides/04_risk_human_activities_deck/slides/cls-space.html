Space: risk accepted per mission, not designed per hour

Crewed vehicles

1 in 270

loss of crew per ISS mission (NASA Commercial Crew requirement)

  • Ascent ≤ 1 in 500, entry ≤ 1 in 500
  • On orbit, micrometeoroids and debris drive the risk
  • Actual: Shuttle 2 losses in 135 flights; Soyuz 2 fatal flights (1967, 1971)

Uncrewed vehicles

≈ 96 %

of orbital launches reached orbit in 2025 (about 317 of 325); first flights fail far more often

  • Payload classes A–D (NASA NPR 8705.4A): A very low risk (JWST) … D high risk (CubeSats)
  • A class A payload flies on a launcher with a long record
  • A lost probe costs money and years, not lives

NASA Commercial Crew: loss of crew below 1/500 for ascent and 1/500 for entry, and 1/270 for the whole mission including up to 210 days docked at the ISS, where micrometeoroids and orbital debris drive the risk; the Aerospace Safety Advisory Panel doubted the providers would reach 1/500. Soyuz 1 (1967) and Soyuz 11 (1971) are the two fatal Soyuz flights. Uncrewed: 2025 had about 325 orbital launch attempts and 317 successes (BryceTech, 96 %); 2024 had 6 failures in 263 attempts. NASA NPR 8705.4A: payload classes A (very low risk) to D (high risk tolerance); NPD 8610.7 pairs class A payloads with launchers of Risk Category 3 (robust flight history). Passengers: in the US, FAA licences launches and re-entries for public safety; spaceflight participants must give informed consent (14 CFR 460.45).
