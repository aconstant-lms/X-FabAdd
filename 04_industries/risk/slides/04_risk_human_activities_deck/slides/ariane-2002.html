Ariane 5 ECA flight 157: the Vulcain 2 nozzle gives way

  • 11 December 2002, Kourou: first flight of the 10-tonne Ariane 5 ECA, two satellites on board, no crew
  • A leak opens in the cooling circuit of the Vulcain 2 nozzle extension; the nozzle overheats and loses its integrity
  • Asymmetric thrust: control of the trajectory is lost, about 3 minutes after lift-off; the launcher is destroyed

Most probable cause (Inquiry Board, January 2003)

  • Fissures in the cooling tubes that form the nozzle structure: degraded thermal state
  • Flight loads on the Vulcain 2 not defined in full, and hard to reproduce in ground firings
  • Differences from Vulcain 1 (12 successful flights): the shape of the cooling tubes and the stiffener technology

What changed

  • Nozzle redesigned with the Vulcain 1 experience
  • Ground simulation of the loads seen in flight
  • Better quality of flight equipment; ECA back in 2005
  • Rosetta, due on an Ariane 5 in January 2003, flew in 2004 to another comet

Lesson: a single nozzle with no backup is a Class A part even with no one on board; it was qualified on the ground for loads that only flight produced. Define the loads before the class can be trusted.

Source: Arianespace / ESA, Flight 157 Inquiry Board findings, 7 January 2003

Arianespace press release of 7 January 2003 (ESA): the Inquiry Board, chaired by Wolfgang Koschel, found a leak in the Vulcain 2 nozzle cooling circuit during the first flight phase, followed by critical overheating of the nozzle and loss of its integrity, a major thrust imbalance and loss of trajectory control. Most probable cause: the simultaneous occurrence of a degraded thermal condition of the nozzle due to fissures in the cooling tubes, and a non-exhaustive definition of the loads to which the Vulcain 2 engine is subjected in flight, difficult to simulate in ground tests. The Vulcain 1 and Vulcain 2 nozzles differ in the shape of the cooling tubes forming the structure and in the technology of the stiffeners. Recommendations accepted: modify the Vulcain 2 nozzle with the Vulcain 1 experience, assess ground simulation of flight loads, improve flight equipment quality. Payloads: Hot Bird 7 and Stentor. Rosetta was rescheduled from January 2003 and launched in March 2004 towards comet 67P. Class view: uncrewed, so the loss is a mission and financial one, but the nozzle is a single item without redundancy, the equivalent of Class A for the vehicle.
