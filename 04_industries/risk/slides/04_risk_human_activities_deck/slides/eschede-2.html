Eschede: a fatigue crack inside a worn tyre

Fracture surface: origin on the inner face, arrest lines (Richard et al. 2005)

> 3.5

fatigue safety factor at Ø 862 mm, 80 kN

Fracture mechanics (Richard 2005)

  • Highest hoop stress on the hidden inner face
  • A 1.5 mm crack grows slowly, with arrests, long before rupture

Full-scale tests (Kubota 2009)

  • No fretting damage from the rubber contact
  • Test crack starts at the centre of the inner face, as in service

Lesson: the design margin looked sufficient; the missing barrier was an inspection able to see the inner face. ICE 1 went back to monobloc wheels.

EXED Polytechnique - Additive Manufacturing - 04 Industries by Andrei Constantinescu 2027

Richard et al. 2005: new tyre diameter 920 mm, broken wheel 862 mm, limit 854 mm; tyre thickness from 60 to 31 mm; wheel force Q = 1.25 Q0 = 98 kN (Q0 = 78 kN static); highest circumferential stress at the inner side of the worn tyre for straight running; calculated fatigue safety sufficient for new and re-profiled tyres, crack initiation 'most likely an incidental event'; an initial crack of 1.5 mm depth grows, discontinuously, for a long time before final rupture. Kubota and Hirakawa 2009: no typical fretting damage under rubber contact; maximum hoop stress at the centre of the inner surface; safety factor above 3.5 for 862 mm under 80 kN; the full-scale wheel failed at about 1.56 million cycles under 308 kN with the crack at the centre of the inner surface. After Eschede, ICE 1 trains returned to monobloc wheels with ultrasonic inspection.
