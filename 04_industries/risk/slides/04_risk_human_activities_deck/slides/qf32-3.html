Lesson: the failure analysis assumed that every part conforms to its drawing. A low-class part made out of tolerance started a chain that ended at a Class A part. Classify along the whole failure chain, and match manufacturing control to the class.

ATSB AO-2010-089, sections 2.4.5 and 2.4.6; JAR-E: extremely remote ≤ 10⁻⁸ per engine flying hour

ATSB AO-2010-089 sections 2.4.5-2.4.6: the engine manufacturer classified parts as Critical (primary failure likely to have hazardous effects, requiring special controls), Reliability sensitive, or Unclassified. The Trent 900 FMECA identified the HP/IP bearing support assembly as Unclassified; the post-event review found it inappropriately classified and reclassified it Reliability sensitive. The FMECA recorded a failure mode very similar to the stub pipe failure, ending in IP turbine drive failure and overspeed above burst speed, classified hazardous with the acceptable probability extremely remote (JAR-E: not greater than 1e-8 per engine flying hour), assuming all parts conformed to the design. Class letters A to D are the course scale; the mapping of the manufacturer's classes onto it is indicative.
