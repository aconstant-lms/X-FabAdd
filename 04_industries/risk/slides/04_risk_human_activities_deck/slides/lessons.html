CONCLUSION

Two routes to catastrophe

A Class A part fails

Sioux City · Eschede · Ariane 5 ECA · Adamuz

  • A hidden material or manufacturing defect in a single load path
  • Calculations were right for the part as drawn, not for the part as made
  • Answer: damage tolerance, cleaner material processes, inspection able to see the defect

A lower class or management starts it

Qantas 32 · Columbia · Challenger · Gelida

  • An unclassified pipe, foam insulation or an external wall starts the chain
  • Known anomalies are accepted flight after flight; constraints are waived
  • Answer: classify along the whole failure chain, and never waive a Class A constraint

Estimated risk ≠ real risk: 1 in 100 000 claimed, 1 in 68 observed on the Shuttle.

One event defeats redundancy: debris cut every hydraulic line (Sioux City) and wiring (Qantas 32).

Two routes to catastrophe. A Class A part can fail by itself from a defect that nobody could see (Sioux City, Eschede, Adamuz). Or a lower-class item, an external structure or a management decision starts a chain that ends at a Class A part (Qantas 32, Columbia, Challenger, Gelida). The class of a part decides how much evidence is needed about its material, its manufacturing process and its inspection; the class must be set along the failure chain.
