# Executive day: introduction to additive manufacturing for leaders

EXED Polytechnique — Additive Manufacturing, by Andrei Constantinescu 2027.
Planning notes from the discussion of 30–31 October 2026. Checked against public sources on those dates;
figures marked "to check" before use.

Audience: executive master participants from finance, medical, law, engineering and commerce.
Format: one day, 8 hours (8:30–17:30).

Tables reused below: `tables/00_films.csv`, `tables/00_data_sources.csv`.

---

## 1. Guiding idea

Additive manufacturing (AM) is a business decision as much as a technology. Each module answers a
question a leader in the room would ask. Technology stays in one compact block; most of the day goes to
applications, economics, risk and a cross-functional exercise.

Design principles:

- **Mixed tables from the start**: one person each from finance, medical, law, engineering and commerce.
  The afternoon workshop depends on these groups.
- **Something to touch**: pass printed parts around (lattice, consolidated assembly, dental aligner mould,
  titanium implant sample).
- **No film longer than 10 minutes**, at most one per module; a film opens a discussion, it does not replace it.
- **Every module ends with "so what for your sector?"**

## 4. Data and cases

See `tables/00_data_sources.csv` for the full list with URLs.

**Market (opening, finance module)**

- Wohlers Report 2026: global AM revenues $24.2 B in 2025, +10.9 % (well below the 20 %+ pre-pandemic rates);
  services 48 %, machines and servicing 26 %, materials 20 %, software 6 %. Paid report; headline figures are public.
- AMPOWER Report 2026: industrial AM > €11 B in 2025, +5.6 %; forecast 13.5 % CAGR to > €21 B in 2030; defence a key driver.
  Compare with Wohlers: the gap comes from the market definition (exercise for finance participants).
- AM Research: $4.48 B in Q2 2026, +12 % year on year.
- Free exercise: five-year share-price charts of listed AM companies (Stratasys, 3D Systems, SPAC-era entrants) to show the hype cycle.

**Cases with numbers (applications, workshop)**

- Deutsche Bahn spare parts: > 200,000 printed parts in service, > €20 M saved since 2015; started 2015 with a plastic coat hook;
  digital warehouse ≈ 1,000 models, target 10,000 by 2030. Basis for the "digitise obsolete spare parts" scenario
  (see also `04_industries/rail/`).
- GE / CFM LEAP fuel nozzle: one printed part instead of ≈ 20 welded and brazed parts; serial production in Auburn from 2015;
  100,000th nozzle shipped 2021.
- Relativity Space: cautionary case on ambition, capital and execution (Terran 1: 85 % printed by mass, first launch failed short of orbit).
- Vendor case libraries (EOS, Stratasys, HP, Materialise, Carbon): best-case numbers; exercise "what is missing from this vendor case?".
- Teaching cases: search Harvard Business Publishing and The Case Centre for "additive manufacturing" / "3D printing" (check dates).
- Martens, Fan & Dwyer (2020), Dutch SMEs: identify opportunities → experiment → embed. Framework for the closing checklist.

**Law, IP, regulation (risk module)**

- EPO "Innovation trends in additive manufacturing" (2023): international patent families +26.3 %/yr 2013–2020, ≈ 8× all fields;
  top applicants GE, Raytheon Technologies, HP. EPO 2020 study: health sector leads AM patent filings since 2010.
- FDA guidance "Technical Considerations for Additive Manufactured Medical Devices"; EU MDR custom-made devices.
- ISO/ASTM 52900 series (terminology, process categories).
- Export controls: EU dual-use regulation, US ITAR / EAR on technical data (a CAD file can be a controlled export).

**Background news**: Metal AM magazine (free archive), VoxelMatters, TCT Magazine, 3D Printing Industry;
Deloitte "3D Opportunity" series and free course (frameworks still valid, figures dated).

Workshop tip: one-page data sheet per case (numbers, timeline, two or three open questions) rather than full articles.
